# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Primary audience is recruiters and hiring managers, arriving with limited time to decide whether a candidate is worth reaching out to. Secondary audience is peers and collaborators in the developer/research community assessing credibility. The context is a quick scan, not a deep read: the portfolio has to earn attention fast and make the case for contact before the visitor moves on.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio for Paula Gallucci Zurita — software engineer, networking instructor at Universitat de Lleida, and researcher at Eurecat — built on the Questfolio framework's gamified presentation of a developer's work: XP, levels, achievements, and unlockable projects instead of a static list. Success is a recruiter or collaborator walking away convinced of her technical credibility and reaching out directly.

## Positioning

The gamification itself is the differentiator, not a decoration on top of an otherwise ordinary portfolio. Projects, achievements, and progress framed as a game (quests, XP, locked/unlocked states, trophies) is what makes this memorable next to a typical dev portfolio — but that framing only works if the substance underneath (real teaching, research, and engineering work) holds up under it.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: contact Paula directly via the email and social links already surfaced in the profile.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: this engineer turned her portfolio into a game — and the work behind it is real.
- Belief ladder: (1) this looks different from every other dev portfolio, (2) the gamification isn't just a gimmick — the projects, achievements, and timeline show credible, real work, (3) this person is worth reaching out to now.
- Proof on hand: the content itself — teaching role at Universitat de Lleida, research role at Eurecat, and the projects/achievements/timeline entries. No separate testimonials or press supplied.

## Brand Personality

Sleek and technical — precise, confident, cyberpunk/terminal-native rather than playful-for-its-own-sake. The existing dark neon theme (Cyber Nebula) already leans this direction; the gamification mechanics should read as engineered and deliberate, not cute.

## Anti-references

None specified.

## Design Principles

Gamification must feel earned, not gimmicky: XP, achievements, and locked content exist to make credible work memorable, not to disguise thin content. Every screen should lead with technical credibility and let playfulness follow, never the reverse. The path to contact should stay one step away no matter which page a recruiter lands on first. Locked or pending content (in-progress projects, unearned achievements) should read as building curiosity, not as broken or incomplete. The portfolio's own execution — performance, polish, motion — is itself proof of engineering quality; practice what it preaches.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No formal requirements specified. Given the dark, saturated-accent theme (Cyber Nebula), contrast should still be checked in practice as the visual system develops.
